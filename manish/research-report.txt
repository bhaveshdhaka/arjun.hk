COACHING PHILOSOPHY

Manish's own concise public positioning is: "I Help People Conquer Pain, Not Just Manage It." This can be expressed as a commitment to building capacity and restoring confidence in movement, but it should not be framed as a cure or medical guarantee.

ATG's published training framework supplies useful context for presenting the coaching approach. Sources:
- https://www.atgforcoaches.com/
- https://www.atgonlinecoaching.com/articles/the-10-atg-principles
- https://www.atgonlinecoaching.com/articles/article-knee-ability-zero-basics-and-pro

ATG's published principles include:
1. Build From the Ground Up
2. Build From Side-to-Side
3. Build From Front-to-Back
4. Build Both Short-Range and Long-Range
5. Build From Light-to-Heavy
6. Build From Long-to-Short (Duration)
7. Build From Slow-to-Fast
8. Build and Keep a Bulletproofing Reserve
9. Truth in Numbers
10. Pain-Free Sets the Gains Free

ATG describes these as measurable, scalable movement progressions. Its published Knee Ability framework includes scaled progressions and lower-body movements such as sled/resisted-treadmill work, step-ups, ATG Split Squats, deep squats, tibialis raises, calf work, hamstring/Nordic work, mobility, hips, and core. These describe ATG's framework; they are not proof that every movement or programme is appropriate for every person.

Website-ready, appropriately scoped philosophy copy:
"Manish's coaching combines strength training with progressive, baseline-aware movement work. Drawing on the ATG progression framework, sessions can be scaled for a client's current capacity, using measurable progress in range, control, load, and speed."

Avoid outcome guarantees including "cures pain", "prevents injury", "rehabilitates injuries", or "guaranteed performance gains" unless appropriately supported and legally reviewed.
