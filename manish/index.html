Credential context: ATG Level 3

ATG For Coaches publicly describes a tiered coach-certification membership and characterises Level 3 as elite status. The official programme provides education around the ATG system and a coach-discovery map. Its public materials do not document Level 3’s assessment criteria, supervised hours, examination, recertification requirements, or independent accreditation status.

Accurate website copy: “Manish Singh is an ATG Level 3 Certified Coach, listed in ATG’s official coach directory.” Link the credential to the official directory profile.

Do not infer clinical status. This credential should not be expanded into medical, physiotherapy, diagnostic, injury-treatment, or guaranteed-outcome claims without separate evidence and appropriate professional review.

ATG-informed training framework

ATG’s official materials describe a progression-based approach that measures and scales range, load, duration, speed, and complexity. The principles below are ATG’s framework, included to provide useful context—not a claim that any programme is universally appropriate.

  1. Build From the Ground Up
  2. Build From Side-to-Side
  3. Build From Front-to-Back
  4. Build Both Short-Range and Long-Range
  5. Build From Light-to-Heavy
  6. Build From Long-to-Short (Duration)
  7. Build From Slow-to-Fast
  8. Build and Keep a Bulletproofing Reserve
  9. Truth in Numbers
  10. Pain-Free Sets the Gains Free

ATG’s published Knee Ability material discusses scaled progressions and movements including sled/resisted-treadmill work, step-ups, ATG Split Squats, deep squats, tibialis raises, calf work, hamstring/Nordic work, mobility, hips, and core.

Primary context: The 10 ATG Principles and Knee Ability Zero, Basics & Pro.
